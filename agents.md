# Worth Looking Up agent guide

## Purpose and principles
Help people know when the sky is worth going outside for, from their location. Preserve honest
uncertainty, responsiveness, local ownership and both themes. All paths, deployment names and
repository identifiers must be lowercase. Product name: Worth Looking Up. This file is lowercase
because of that rule; agents should read it explicitly (some tools discover only AGENTS.md).

## Architecture and source of truth
- index.html owns UI, site/timezone selection, forecast orchestration, bounded persistence.
- overhead-engine.js owns orbital math and scoring; overhead-worker.js hosts calculations.
- overhead-lifecycle.js controls current/recent/eligible event timing across every presentation.
- overhead-weekly.js and overhead-trains.js interpret opportunities without modifying SGP4.
- overhead-calendar.js is the only iCalendar writer: a pure module (no DOM, storage, network or engine
  call) that turns a discovery event into RFC 5545 text, with UTC instants, stable product-owned UIDs
  and explicit refusals for anything the source cannot time. index.html owns the download, the
  session-only observing plan and every user-facing label. See docs/calendar.md.
- overhead-objects.js enriches selected objects; metadata failures must not affect ranking.
- overhead-astro.js wraps pinned Astronomy Engine; planets/meteors/comets feed opportunities and
  celestial-plan; overhead-celestial-worker.js executes the same plan off the main thread.
- data/overhead/*.json and relevant fixtures are pipeline output: change tools/overhead_*.py,
  validate, regenerate atomically. Never hand-edit facts or replace automated refresh commits.
- Retained Overhead globals/schema/storage names are compatibility identifiers, not host coupling.
- Future launches: src/launches (schedule engine, feed schema and the Phase 2 visibility design);
  overhead-launches.js interprets schedules for the browser; pages-worker.mjs serves the feed and
  scripts/refresh-launches.mjs refreshes it on a schedule. Launch schedules never enter satellite or
  celestial scoring, and local visibility is always labelled unknown without flight geometry.
- src/launches/launch-trajectory.mjs owns ascent geometry and the five-level evidence taxonomy:
  no DOM/storage/network, no ephemeris of its own and no import of the schedule engine. It may never
  publish a brightness, plume-size, probability or visibility claim. Its research fixtures come from
  scripts/build-trajectory-fixtures.mjs (public-domain webcast transcriptions) and its inventory is
  src/launches/trajectory-sources.json; see docs/launch-trajectory-feasibility.md. A profile whose values
  are not anchored to an achieved orbit may support a direction, never an elevation.
- Phase 5B publishes reviewed historical profiles in data/launches/trajectory-profiles.json (schema
  worthlookingup.trajectory-profiles/1), generated by scripts/build-trajectory-profiles.mjs and documented
  in docs/trajectory-profiles.md and docs/phase-5b-release.md. A profile may produce an elevation or a
  timed window only when both anchoring gates pass — plane consistency (with the derived-azimuth case
  recorded as not applicable, never as a pass) and terminal-state consistency against a catalogued
  insertion orbit by vis-viva at the reconstructed geocentric radius. A failed gate can only downgrade
  a profile to direction-only; it must never be overridden, and the same rules are enforced by the
  generator, by validateProfileArtifact at read time and by the tests. overhead-launch-explorer.js is the
  only product-facing adapter: it loads the staged module and the artifact on demand, calls
  exploreHistorical() and renders the result, performs no geometry of its own and refuses to attempt a
  load on a page that cannot serve modules. Nothing may attach a historical profile to an upcoming launch
  by vehicle family, launch site or a hazard polygon, and local visibility stays unknown everywhere.
- Phase 5C keeps that capability honest and cheap, in four parts. (1) The observer-facing geometry lives in
  src/launches/launch-geodesy.mjs, which launch-trajectory.mjs imports and re-exports unchanged: the launch
  calendar may load the geometry without the evidence machinery, and there must never be a second geometry
  model. (2) The launch calendar reads the generated compact index data/launches/trajectory-pad-index.json
  (schema worthlookingup.trajectory-pad-index/1, validated by src/launches/launch-pad-index.mjs) instead of
  the profile artifact; the artifact and the evidence module load only when a reader opens the historical
  panel. The index carries ids, dates, evidence levels, output classes and the artifact's sha256 — never a
  sample, an elevation or an azimuth — and a missing, invalid or stale index can only remove a link.
  (3) data/launches/trajectory-review.json (schema worthlookingup.trajectory-review/1) is the only way a
  published output may be suspended: it is added by hand after the monthly audit, it never rewrites a
  recorded gate result, and an entry in a record that does not validate suppresses nothing. (4) The
  evidence audit (scripts/audit-trajectory-profiles.mjs, judgement in src/launches/trajectory-audit.mjs,
  workflow .github/workflows/trajectory-evidence-audit.yml, policy in docs/trajectory-audit.md) is
  read-only and monthly: it never promotes a profile, never demotes one automatically, never writes the
  published files, and never gates the hourly launch or celestial workflows. src/launches/mission-direction.mjs
  is a contract only (five direction classes, of which only a published or reconstructed mission-specific
  direction may ever reach an upcoming mission's sector); nothing in the product imports it, the deployment
  does not ship it, and tests assert both.

- Phase 6A adds the Milky Way as a first-class sixth category, and keeps it honest in four ways.
  `overhead-milkyway.js` is a pure module (no DOM/storage/network) that places the galactic centre — one
  fixed J2000 catalogue direction, Sgr A* — with the same pinned engine and `horizontalFromJ2000` every
  other body uses, computes the core's altitude, azimuth, transit, rise/set and above-threshold intervals,
  intersects them with the existing twilight intervals, samples the Moon inside the window, and reports the
  geometry of the *galactic plane* separately so a low core is never reported as an invisible band. It may
  never claim brightness, sky brightness, light pollution, transparency, a plume or a sighting probability,
  and it must never describe a hazard, a corridor or a family history as a direction. Darkness is solar
  altitude, moonlight is the Moon's own position and illuminated fraction (a bright Moon below the horizon
  costs nothing), and weather is reported separately with its forecast range and age: missing weather is
  never clear skies. The window quality is the category's own documented, monotone formula (45/30/25 on
  core altitude, darkness and moonlight), is not comparable with satellite, meteor or planetary observing
  values, and says so wherever it appears. The category is the last tab, contributes Tonight and Coming Up
  events with `precision: 'window'` through the shared discovery mapper, and exports to the calendar as an
  interval through overhead-calendar.js — never as a one-hour block. No calculation or dataset request
  happens until a reader opens the category. See docs/milky-way.md.
- Phase 6A.1 adds the **next season**, for readers whose next opportunity is beyond the ninety-day plan.
  `analyseSeason()` in the same engine walks the site's own observing nights in date order through the
  unchanged `analyseNight()` and stops at the first hit, which is exact because time order is the question;
  the bound is 366 nights and no pre-filter exists, because a conservative pre-filter is how an off-by-one-night
  bug hides. Two dates are kept apart: the **season opening** (geometry — the first night the window exists) and
  the **first favourable night** (the first night whose own window quality reaches 60, a bar measured from a full
  year at the reference sites, never a new scale and never a cross-category comparison). A negative answer is
  classified by cause — never reaches the threshold, below the threshold, no darkness, no
  darkness overlap, unavailable — and every one keeps the band statement: out of reach for the core is not
  invisible for the galaxy. A night is labelled by its evening and a window by the date of its own instant
  (`day` versus `date`, `Intl` in the site's zone), so daylight saving and pre-dawn windows stay correct.
  Weather months ahead is `not-forecastable`: no cloud, brightness, probability or "sighting chance" may appear
  in the record or in any exported file. The search is on demand in its own worker request
  (`kind: 'season'`), cached per site/generation/threshold, and makes no network request; a future window enters
  the ordinary opportunity, discovery and calendar machinery through `seasonCandidate()`, and the global
  planning horizons must never be stretched to a year to accommodate it. See docs/milky-way.md.
- Phase 6B.1 is **research only**: `src/geodata/*.mjs` is a pure evaluation layer (no DOM, storage, network or
  engine call) and `research/geodata/` holds the candidate inventory, DEM-derived horizon profiles, generators
  and a self-contained report. None of it is staged by `scripts/build.cjs`, imported by the page or published;
  `tests/geodata-sites.cjs` asserts that `dist/` contains neither the modules nor any research artefact. The
  three dimensions stay separate and **no composite score, rank or travel time may be computed**: sky quality,
  terrain clearance and access are reported as three verdicts with their evidence. Sky brightness is currently
  `unavailable` everywhere because no dataset clears the gate, and `unavailable` is never rendered as dark.
  Upward radiance (VIIRS, nW cm^-2 sr^-1) is never converted into sky brightness; the only conversion allowed is
  mcd/m^2 to mag/arcsec^2 with the stated zero point, and the natural sky must be added before any total is
  quoted. No Bortle class may be derived from any of it. The licensing gate in `src/geodata/geo-sources.mjs`
  computes eligibility from `src/geodata/sources.json` and throws if an entry overstates its own eligibility;
  a source is production-eligible only when its licence permits commercial use and redistribution, it carries
  no unresolved share-alike obligation, and a job can read it. Access is decided by `access-evidence.mjs`:
  only `verified-night-access` may ever support an affirmative recommendation, a restriction always wins over a
  permission, a third-party map can never verify anything, a permission without a documented legal parking place
  is not enough, and verification expires after a year and is then downgraded rather than kept. A DEM is bare
  earth: a low modelled horizon is never a clear view, and every terrain claim carries that caveat plus an
  uncertainty. See docs/photography-geodata-feasibility.md.
- Phase 6B.2 ships the **Where to go** explorer, and it is area-only on purpose. `data/geodata/sites.json` is a
  generated static artifact (`scripts/build-geodata-sites.mjs`, `npm run geodata:sites:check` in `npm test`) built
  from the Phase 6B.1 inventory, the simplified PAD-US outlines in `research/geodata/boundaries/` and the
  hand-maintained quotations in `src/geodata/access-verifications.json`. **Only areas whose after-dark access is
  documented by the managing agency are published**, and a parcel centroid must never be promoted to an
  observation point, trailhead, gate or parking place: every record is `type: 'area'` with an outline, no record
  carries coordinates, terrain is regional context only (a parcel has no single horizon), and distance is
  straight-line to the nearest mapped edge, labelled as a management boundary rather than an entrance or a drive.
  The artifact validator and the generator both refuse coordinates, parking, trailheads, clearance angles, travel
  times, Bortle classes, sky brightness and scores, and a test proves no excluded candidate appears in the public
  bytes. Sky brightness stays `unavailable` with the sentence "Local artificial sky brightness has not yet been
  assessed for these locations"; upward radiance is never converted and no darkness rating is ever derived.
  `overhead-sites.js` and the artifact load only when a reader opens the section, the comparison list is the
  authoritative interface and the map is a tile-free SVG sketch beside it, and the window shown is always the
  visitor's own because no area has a verified observation point. Access evidence is re-checked by hand, carries
  its URL, quotation and date, expires after a year, and must never be replaced by a crowdsourced or generic
  ownership signal. See docs/phase-6b2-regional-site-explorer.md.

## Coding and behavior
No framework rewrite, bundler or speculative services. Prefer pure modules and browser APIs.
Guard storage and external requests; keep fallback paths, timestamps and stale warnings.
Escape text interpolated into HTML. No invented events, brightness, rarity, accuracy or precision.
Changes to astronomy/scoring require deterministic fixture evidence and documented rationale;
this migration's engines match docs/source-manifest.json. Update that baseline deliberately only
when a later justified engine change is accepted. Never loosen scientific tolerances to pass tests.

## Checks
npm ci; npm test; npm run test:browser. Browser tests use Chrome locally, Chromium with CI=1.
Python dependencies: requirements.txt. Run precision test and both builders --check plus comet
--no-network for pipeline changes. Live-provider tests are opt-in; report skips honestly.
Run npm run build and verify the staged artifact for releases. Worker imports, missing assets,
origin independence, transfer validation and deployment packaging are checked in tests/migration.cjs.
Never replace a real behavior test with an assertion mirroring its implementation.
Hourly refresh workflows validate their own artifact, engine and endpoint only: unrelated regression
suites must never gate data publication. They push only after fetching and replaying on origin/main and
never force. Test fixtures for continuously refreshed artifacts are fixed records declared in the test;
only content-independent invariants may be asserted against the live artifact.

## Git synchronization and releases
Inspect status/branch/remotes; fetch origin before work and before push. Main is shared with a
scheduled data bot. Fast-forward only when safe; reconcile genuine divergence preserving work.
Never force-push, reset, clean, automatically stash or discard user/untracked/automated changes.
Initial empty remote is allowed. Push normally, then fetch and verify origin/main contains HEAD.
Use scripts/git-sync.sh for ordinary established clones. Explicitly report failed fetches.
Version index.html comment, footer attribute and visible text together as YYYY.MM.DD.N when
shipping UI changes. CI and production checks must inspect deployed content, not infer from push.

## Sources, licensing, storage
Keep direct CelesTrak/Open-Meteo/Wikidata requests unless a specific issue warrants a cache/API.
Respect terms, limits and attribution; free API access does not grant commercial redistribution.
Review docs/providers.md before monetization. No unlicensed photos; assets/provenance.json records
origin/rights. Keep vendor license notices. Do not silently grant a license to extracted source.
Preferences use overhead.* localStorage on the independent origin. Downloaded responses use
IndexedDB overhead-cache (32 responses, 7 days), identity uses overhead-object-meta (200, 21d).
Never delete user sites/favorites to free caches. Transfers are explicit, validated, bounded,
allowlisted; never transfer analytics IDs or downloaded caches. Preserve the old origin's data.
